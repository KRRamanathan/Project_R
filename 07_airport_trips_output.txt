3. EFFECTIVE ECONOMICS PER COMPLETED TRIP-CYCLE
====================================================================================================
  Completed = captain_cancelled is False. Return flag on cancelled trips is
  not a completed cycle; those rows are out of this section.
  Sampled: compare means/rates, do not scale to market totals.

  Deadhead proxy (declared):
    Loaded yield ₹/km = fare_inr / trip_distance_km on completed trips.
    Median yield across completed trips is the ₹/km charged to empty km.
    If got_return: implied deadhead km = 0 on this cycle (next fare in 20 min).
    If not: implied deadhead km = trip_distance_km (empty return of similar length).
    implied_deadhead_cost = implied_deadhead_km × median_yield.
    net_cycle = fare_inr − implied_deadhead_cost.
  Limitation: inbound/return fare is not in the file, so got_return cycles are
  credited only by avoiding deadhead, not by adding a second fare. That UNDERSTATES
  the advantage of getting a return. Sensitivity: expected_gross = fare × (1+got_return)
  as if the return paid the same as the outbound.

  suburban has the worst return economics → that explains overnight airport
  no-shows. Part 1 (mix dominance / mix shift) FAILS: city_core is at least as
  large overnight (see shares), suburban share is indistinguishable from

  city_core/tech_park, and net_cycle ₹ is the lowest slice.
  Also: return-in-20m falls overnight in EVERY drop type (~8–9pp), so the night
  penalty is general, not suburban-only.
  What the data DO support: a large, all-day suburban backhaul problem plus a
  general overnight return-fare collapse. What they do NOT support: that
  overnight airport-origin trips become suburban-heavy. Do not hang Step 6
  solely on an overnight mix shift that is not in this sample.
