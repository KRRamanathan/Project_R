2. ELASTICITY — online_captains vs avg_surge_multiplier at airport
====================================================================================================
  Identification caveat: surge is set in response to imbalance. A negative
  correlation means 'price is high when supply is already missing' — that is
  the shortage itself, not a supply curve. Hour-of-day FE uses within-hour
  variation. Even then, reverse causality remains. We interpret the SIGN:
  if captains do not rise with surge in the worst hours, supply is inelastic
  to the prices already on the table (non-price constraint).

====================================================================================================
PRICE PROBLEM VS NON-PRICE CONSTRAINT
====================================================================================================
  In the highest-loss airport hours:
    unfulfilled% is high, avg_surge is already ~2.09x,
    online_captains sit at ~12.9 vs ~35.4 in other hours,
    and ETA is elevated in lockstep.
  Captains move *against* surge in the raw data (shortage pricing).
  After hour-of-day controls, a large positive captains-on-surge slope would have
  been evidence that supply still has room to respond to price. We do not see
  that in the worst hours (see coefficients above).

  Diagnosis: NON-PRICE CONSTRAINT in the worst hours.
  Surge is already high and supply is not coming. This does not look like
  'raise surge further and airport supply will appear.' It looks like captains
  are not at the terminals overnight — positioning, deadhead, or willingness
  to sit at the airport — which Step B (trips) can test. Not computed here.

  Not claimed: causal elasticity. Simultaneous determination of surge and
  supply is not identified from a single OLS. The claim that survives is
  descriptive: in the hours that hold most of the unfulfilled airport volume,
  price is already high and headcount is already low.
